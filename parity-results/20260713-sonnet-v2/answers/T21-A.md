**The problem:** "No repro in March" was true of March's investigation. It is not evidence that the symptom doesn't exist — it's evidence we didn't find the trigger *at the time*. Fourteen new tickets with the *same symptom* in a single month is a strong signal in the opposite direction: either the trigger condition is real and recurring (maybe tied to a deploy, a retry path, a specific payment provider, a load/concurrency condition we didn't hit in testing, or a subset of customers/plans), or it's became more frequent for a reason we haven't looked at yet. Telling Support to stop escalating duplicate-*billing* complaints — i.e., customers being charged twice, real money — without re-opening the investigation is the kind of call that looks fine right up until it's a chargeback spike, a compliance complaint, or a press-worthy "company charged me twice and ignored me" thread. I'd want to be very sure before signing my name to "known non-issue."

1. Ask Support for the raw ticket list (customer IDs, timestamps, amounts, payment method) for those 14 — not just "same symptom" by description. Duplicate-charge complaints get lumped together in support tools even when the root causes differ (real double-charge vs. pending-vs-settled confusion vs. a UI showing two line items for one charge). We should know what we're actually looking at before we say anything conclusive.
2. Diff those 14 against what March's investigation actually covered — what environment, what payment path, what date range, what repro steps were tried.
