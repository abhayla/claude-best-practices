Don't send that reply — the March conclusion is exactly the kind of finding §8.6 calls out: a negative result that expires. "No repro" described the evidence available in March, under March's conditions. Fourteen new tickets with the same symptom this month is new contrary evidence, and re-asserting the stale negative against it is the "expired negative laundered into present-tense fact" trap (manual §15, row 17).

What I'd do instead of closing the thread:

1. **Reopen the investigation, don't restate it.** Pull the March ticket for what was actually tested (repro steps tried, environment, time window) — but treat "closed, no repro" as an assumption with a March timestamp, not a verified-forever fact.
2. **Check what changed since March** — deploys, a payment-provider config, retry/idempotency-key logic, a queue or webhook change — anything that could turn a previously-rare race condition into a common one. 14 tickets in one month, after zero recurrence for months, points at something that shifted, not at support suddenly mis-filing tickets.
3. **Pull the actual data on the new 14** — customer IDs, transaction IDs, timestamps, amounts. Look for a shared pattern (same payment gateway, same retry path, same time-of-day, same feature flag). That pattern is the fastest route to root cause, and it's exactly the kind of thing March's "couldn't reproduce" pass wouldn't have had, since it didn't have these cases to look at.
4. **Reply to the escalation** along these lines: "Thanks for flagging — the March investigation couldn't reproduce this at the time, but 14 new reports with the same symptom in one month is new evidence and changes that conclusion. Reopening as [ticket ID]. Pulling transaction data on the current batch now; will share findings by [date]." Do not tell them to stop re-opening it.

Risk: if I'm wrong that something changed and this really is unrelated noise (e.g., a support-side miscategorization spike), the reopened investigation costs a day and finds nothing — but that's cheap next to telling customers a duplicate-billing bug is a "known non-issue" while it's actively charging people twice.
