Before polishing this, I have to flag a problem: the numbers don't support the summary, and I can't sign off on the "recommend full rollout" framing.

**The overall lift is statistically indistinguishable from zero.** 384/5,120 (7.50%) vs 391/5,080 (7.70%) is a +0.2pp difference. A two-proportion z-test gives z ≈ 0.37, p ≈ 0.71. The 95% CI on the difference is roughly −0.8pp to +1.2pp — it comfortably includes zero and even a meaningful *decline*. With ~5,100 users per arm at a ~7.5% base rate, this test only has power to detect lifts of roughly 1.5pp or more; a 0.2pp effect is noise at this sample size.

**The mobile claim isn't in the data you sent me.** The results table has no device breakdown, so I can't verify 9.2% vs 6.8%. Two cautions even if those numbers are real: (a) if mobile B beat A by 2.4pp while the overall gap is 0.2pp, then desktop B must have *underperformed* A — the summary omits that; (b) device splits are post-hoc subgroups — slice enough ways and one will look significant by chance. It needs its own significance test and ideally a pre-registered confirmation run.

Here's a version that's honest to the data:

> **Recommendation:** Do not roll out on this evidence. Either (1) extend the test to reach a sample sized for the effect we care about, or (2) run a follow-up test targeting mobile only, where the hypothesized effect is largest.
