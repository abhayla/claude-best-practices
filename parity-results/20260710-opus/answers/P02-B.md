## The spec is not buildable as written — (a) and (b) directly contradict, and (c) removes the usual escape hatch. Before I draft an approach, we need to pick which constraint gives.

Here is the arithmetic, because it is the whole story:

- Poll once every 60s → an alert that lands **just after** a poll waits nearly a full 60s for the next one. Worst-case detection latency ≈ 60s, average ≈ 30s, before you even add delivery time.
- The SLA is 5s. Polling at 60s misses it by **~12×** in the worst case. There is no scheduling trick that closes that gap: to hit a 5s delivery SLA by polling you need an interval of roughly **2–3s** (leaving headroom for send time), i.e. ~20–30× more frequent than (b) allows.
- Constraint (c) "no push/streaming" is the reason we can't sidestep this the normal way. Event-driven delivery (webhook/SSE/websocket) is exactly how you get sub-5s *without* busy-polling — and it's off the table this quarter.

So: **any two of the three are satisfiable; all three together are not.** I won't hand you a design that quietly satisfies two and papers over the third — that's the kind of spec that ships and then pages someone at 2am when the SLA is missed.

## The three honest options — pick the constraint to relax

**Option 1 — Relax (b): poll every 2–3s.** Meets the 5s SLA, adds no push/streaming infra, satisfies (a)+(c). Cost: more frequent queue reads.
*This is very likely the right answer*, because the premise under (b) is weak — see the cost note below.

**Option 2 — Relax (a): accept ~60–70s delivery.** Keep 60s polling, keep no-push, and rewrite the SLA to match reality (a "notification within ~1 minute" service). Cheapest, but it is a different product than "alerts."

**Option 3 — Relax (c): go event-driven.** A webhook/queue-notify push gets you sub-5s *and* low cost (no busy loop). Best on latency and cost, but it's the thing explicitly deferred this quarter.

## The one question I need answered

**What is the actual queue technology, and does "no push/streaming" forbid a blocking/long-poll receive?**

This matters because there may be a fourth option that threads the needle. A **blocking receive / long-poll** on the existing queue (SQS long-poll, Redis `BLPOP`, Postgres `LISTEN/NOTIFY`) delivers in well under a second, adds no websocket/SSE layer, and isn't a busy poll — so it can satisfy (a) and the cost intent of (b) at once. Whether it counts as "streaming infrastructure" under (c) is a definitional call I can't make for you. If long-poll is allowed, that's my recommendation and I'll spec it. If (c) means "the consumer must be a plain pull with no held connections," it's out and we're back to Options 1–3.

## Risks / what would change my answer

- **The cost premise in (b) is probably a non-bottleneck.** For SQS/Redis/most DB-backed queues, polling every 3s instead of every 60s costs cents, not dollars — a lightweight receive call. Meanwhile missing a 5s alert SLA is expensive. If someone measured a real cost problem with frequent polling, show me the number and I'll re-derive; absent that, Option 1 likely wins outright and this whole conflict dissolves.
- If the 5s is a **soft target** (P95, not hard) rather than "every alert," the trade-space widens and Option 1 with a modest interval is comfortable.
- I've assumed alerts arrive via a queue we consume, and that "delivered" includes send time (email/SMS/webhook-out), which itself eats into the 5s budget — worth confirming so we don't spend the whole budget on detection and blow it on delivery.

Tell me which constraint is soft (my bet: (b)) and the queue tech, and I'll turn the chosen option into a full technical approach in the next pass.
