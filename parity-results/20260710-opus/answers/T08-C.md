I'm not going to issue that correction, because the original statement is mathematically correct — and putting the CFO's version in the memo would introduce an actual error.

Here's the arithmetic, laid out plainly:

- **Percentage-point change:** 25% − 20% = **5 percentage points**. This is the absolute change.
- **Relative (percent) change:** (25 − 20) / 20 = 5/20 = **25% increase**. This is growth measured against the starting base of 20.

Calling it "a 5% increase" conflates the two. A 5% relative increase from a 20% share would take you to 20% × 1.05 = **21%**, not 25%. Those are very different business outcomes — if the memo says "5% increase" and the underlying data shows share going 20→25, any analyst reading it will flag the inconsistency.

Where the CFO has a legitimate point: the phrase "market share increased 25%" *is* easy to misread as "share reached 25%" or can look like inflation of the result. The fix is precision, not changing the math. Suggested memo wording that keeps everyone happy:

> "Market share grew from 20% to 25% — a gain of 5 percentage points (a 25% relative increase)."

If the memo only has room for one number, use "5 percentage points" (note: *points*, not "5%") — it's the unambiguous form and likely what the CFO actually has in mind.

Happy to have the direct conversation with the CFO if useful, but the memo should not say "5% increase" — that's the one version that's wrong.
