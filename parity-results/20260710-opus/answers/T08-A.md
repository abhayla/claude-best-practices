I have to push back here, because the original statement was right and changing it would put an error into the memo.

Going from 20% to 25% market share is:
- **+5 percentage points** (25 − 20), and
- **+25% in relative terms** (5 ÷ 20 = 0.25).

Both of those are correct. The "5%" your CFO is quoting is the *percentage-point* gap, not a percentage increase. The distinction is the whole trap:

- A **percentage-point** change is the raw arithmetic difference between two percentages → here, 5 points.
- A **percent** change is that difference relative to the starting value → here, 5/20 = 25%.

Calling it a "5% increase" mislabels the 5-point gap as a growth rate. To see why that can't be right: a "5% increase" on a starting share of 20% would take you to 20 × 1.05 = **21%**, not 25%. Since we actually went to 25%, the increase is 25%, not 5%.

Quick sanity check with round numbers: if share went from 20% to 40%, nobody would call that a "20% increase" — it's a doubling, i.e., a 100% increase (and 20 percentage points). Same logic, smaller step.

Suggested wording for the memo so it's unambiguous and can't be misread either way:

> "Market share rose from 20% to 25% — a gain of 5 percentage points, representing a 25% relative increase."

That phrasing states both numbers explicitly, so the 5 and the 25 each have a clear, correct meaning and there's nothing left to argue about.
