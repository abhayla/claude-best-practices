**Fact-check: the 14.7% is wrong — it's the simple average, not the annualized return.**

- Total growth: ₹72L / ₹50L = 1.44 → +44% over 3 years. ✓ (that part is fine)
- Where 14.7% came from: 44% ÷ 3 = 14.67% — an arithmetic average, which ignores compounding.
- Correct annualized figure (CAGR): (72/50)^(1/3) − 1 = 1.44^(1/3) − 1 ≈ **12.9% per annum**.

Sanity check: ₹50L compounded at 12.9% → ~56.5 → ~63.7 → ~71.9L ≈ ₹72L. At 14.7% you'd end at ~₹75.4L, not ₹72L.

This matters beyond accuracy: in a fund deck, quoting a simple average as an "annual return" is exactly the kind of overstatement SEBI/regulatory reviewers and sophisticated LPs flag. Investment returns should always be quoted as CAGR (and labeled as such).

**Suggested phrasing:**

> "The portfolio grew from ₹50L to ₹72L over 3 years — a 12.9% CAGR (44% cumulative)."

Or slightly tighter:

> "₹50L → ₹72L in 3 years (12.9% CAGR)."

Two footnote-level caveats worth confirming before it ships: (1) if there were any contributions or withdrawals during the period, even 12.9% is wrong — you'd need a money-weighted return (XIRR) instead; (2) state whether the figure is pre- or post-fees. **Assumption:** ₹50L → ₹72L is a pure start/end value with no interim flows.
