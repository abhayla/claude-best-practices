Hold this one — the copy is wrong on both numbers, and the final price isn't ₹1,100 either.

Stacked discounts multiply, they don't add. The second 20% comes off the already-reduced price, not the original:

- 25% off ₹2,000 → ₹1,500
- 20% off ₹1,500 → **₹1,200**

So:
- **Final price is ₹1,200, not ₹1,100.**
- **Total savings is 40% (₹800), not 45%.**

The "45% total" claim is the classic additive-discount error (25 + 20). Combining two discounts of a% and b% gives 1 − (1−a)(1−b) = 1 − 0.75×0.80 = 40% off — always less than the sum.

Two ways to fix the copy, depending on what's actually true at checkout:

1. **Keep the stacked mechanic, fix the claims:** "Take 25% off, then an extra 20% off at checkout — that's 40% total savings! (₹2,000 → ₹1,200)"
2. **If you genuinely want 45% off / ₹1,100:** you'd need to advertise a flat 45%, or change the second discount. To land exactly at ₹1,100 (45% off) after a 25% cut, the extra would need to be ~26.67% off the ₹1,500 — not clean marketing math, so a flat "45% off, now ₹1,100" reads better.
