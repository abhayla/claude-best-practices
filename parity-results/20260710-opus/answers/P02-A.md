Before drafting a design, I have to flag a hard conflict in the spec — building to it as written would guarantee a missed SLA:

## The three constraints are not jointly satisfiable

- (a) says worst-case delivery ≤ 5s.
- (b) says we check the queue once every 60s.

A once-per-minute poll means an alert that lands one second after a poll waits ~59s before we even pick it up, plus send time. **Worst-case latency is ~60s, which is 12× the 5s SLA.** No amount of downstream optimization fixes this — the polling interval alone sets the floor on latency. So (a) and (b) directly contradict each other. (c) is fine and compatible; I'll keep it.

I'm not going to hand you an approach that quietly fails (a) 98% of the time. Instead, here's what I read the intent to be and how to actually hit it.

**Assumption:** (b) and (c) exist to protect *cost* and to avoid *standing up a push/streaming stack* (WebSockets/SSE/a broker with server-initiated delivery) — not because "60 seconds" itself matters. If that's right, the 60s number is the negotiable one, and we can honor cost + "no push infra" while still meeting 5s. If the real driver is that the queue is external and literally can only be read once a minute (a vendor limit), then (a) is impossible and the SLA must be renegotiated to ~65s — tell me which.

## Recommended approach: long-poll the queue (meets all real goals)

Long-polling is still request/response polling — it is **not** push/streaming infrastructure, so it satisfies (c) and adds no broker/socket layer.

- A worker opens a blocking receive against the queue (SQS long-poll `WaitTimeSeconds=20`, or Postgres `LISTEN/NOTIFY`, or Redis `BLPOP`). The call returns the instant a message arrives — typically **sub-second, well inside 5s** — and otherwise returns empty after the wait and immediately reconnects.
- Cost stays low because an idle long-poll is one held connection, not a busy spin — you are **not** paying for 20 requests/second. This is cheaper than aggressive short polling and far cheaper than a push stack.
- No new infrastructure: reuses the queue you already have.

## Fallback if long-poll isn't available on the current queue: short-interval polling

If the queue can't long-poll, poll every **2–3 seconds** instead of every 60. That still meets 5s, is still plain polling (no push infra, satisfies (c)), and the cost delta versus 60s is small — a lightweight queue-depth check ~20–30×/min is cheap and can be made cheaper by backing off the interval when the queue has been empty. If cost is genuinely the blocker, a **tiered** scheme works: poll the critical-alert queue every 2s, batch everything else per minute.

2. **The queue can only be read once a minute (hard external limit)** → then (a) is physically unachievable and we renegotiate the SLA to ~65s in writing before I build anything.
