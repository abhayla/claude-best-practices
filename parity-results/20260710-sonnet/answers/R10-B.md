Run the pilot. The colleague's argument misapplies YAGNI — it's answering the wrong question.

YAGNI ("You Aren't Gonna Need It") governs whether you *build/adopt functionality into your own system* before a concrete caller needs it — extension points, config knobs, speculative abstractions. A 15-minute pilot isn't that. It's not implementing nested dispatch as production functionality; it's reconnaissance to establish what a capability of your new *standard* platform actually does — its limits, failure modes, and edge behavior — before you're forced to learn that under pressure later. YAGNI has no opinion on "should I understand the tool I just adopted as my baseline."

The tell that this is a category error: "no current workflow needs nesting" is true and irrelevant. You're not deciding whether to *use* nesting in a workflow today (that's a separate, legitimate YAGNI-governed decision — defer it until a concrete workflow benefits). You're deciding whether to understand a core capability of the orchestration primitive your whole team is standardizing on. Those are different questions, and the colleague's argument answers the first while claiming to settle the second.

Cost asymmetry makes this easy: 15 minutes now vs. discovering nesting's constraints (depth limits, context/cost blowup, error propagation) for the first time mid-incident or mid-deadline, after the team has already fully committed to the platform. A low-cost, one-time, bounded pilot that de-risks an irreversible-ish platform bet is exactly where verification effort belongs (spend effort where being wrong is expensive) — and "wrong" here isn't a bug, it's an unknown gap in a standard you're locking in.

One honest counter-argument, tested: if your team's docs/vendor already fully specify nested-dispatch behavior with no ambiguity and zero plausible future need, the pilot's marginal value could be near zero. But "don't even run the pilot" as a blanket rule doesn't hold that condition — it forecloses finding out. Cheap information-gathering during a platform migration is baseline competence, not scope creep.

**Decision:** run the 15-minute pilot; treat "don't build nesting into workflows yet" as the separate, correctly-YAGNI'd decision to make afterward, informed rather than assumed.
