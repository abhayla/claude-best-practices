CASE-TAG: R10

The colleague's argument conflates two different things: whether to *build* nested-dispatch support into current workflows (that's the YAGNI-relevant question) versus whether to *evaluate* the platform's own standard orchestration feature you're explicitly migrating to (that's a due-diligence question, and YAGNI doesn't apply to it).

Reject the argument. Run the pilot. Here's why:

1. **YAGNI governs building speculative capability, not understanding a dependency you're adopting.** You're not being asked to build nesting into a workflow "because it might be useful later" — you're being asked to spend 15 minutes learning how a feature works that your team is about to depend on wholesale. Not knowing a load-bearing capability of your new standard orchestration layer isn't lean, it's a gap.

2. **"No current workflow needs it" is the wrong test for a migration.** The right test is: will you be able to answer questions about nesting when someone asks — a design review, an incident, a future workflow proposal — without re-deriving it from scratch under time pressure? A pilot now is cheap, structured learning; the same knowledge acquired later, mid-incident or mid-design-debate, costs far more than 15 minutes.

3. **Cost asymmetry is stark.** 15 minutes vs. the cost of an uninformed platform decision later — e.g., someone builds an ad-hoc, hand-rolled nested-orchestration workaround because the team didn't realize the platform already does it, or conversely someone reaches for nesting inappropriately because nobody understood its constraints (depth limits, error propagation, cost multiplier, whatever the pilot would reveal). Either failure mode costs way more than the pilot.

4. **This isn't "evaluate every feature exhaustively"** — that would be over-engineering the evaluation itself. It's specifically about a *nested-dispatch capability of the exact feature you're migrating to*, which is core surface area, not a tangential option.

Decision: run the pilot (15 min), write down what you learn (even "nesting works, we don't need it yet, here's the constraint that matters if we ever do"), and only then decide not to adopt it in current workflows. That gives you the YAGNI outcome (don't build it now) with the due-diligence you actually owe on a platform migration (know what you adopted). Skipping the pilot entirely optimizes for saving 15 minutes at the cost of an informed team — a bad trade on a decision this cheap to make correctly.
