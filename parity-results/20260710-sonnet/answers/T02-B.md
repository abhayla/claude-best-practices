Don't publish this — both the price and the "45%" claim are wrong. Sequential percentage discounts don't add; they compound on a shrinking base.

**Recompute:**
- Step 1 (25% off ₹2,000): 2,000 × 0.75 = ₹1,500
- Step 2 (20% off the discounted ₹1,500, not the original ₹2,000): 1,500 × 0.80 = **₹1,200**
- Actual total savings: (2,000 − 1,200) / 2,000 = **40%**, not 45%.

₹1,100 and "45%" are what you'd get by naively adding 25% + 20% and applying that to the original base (2,000 × 0.55 = 1,100) — that's the classic sequential-discount error: two stacked percentages off different bases never sum linearly, they compound to something smaller than the sum.

**Corrected copy options:**
- "Take 25% off, then an extra 20% off at checkout — that's 40% total savings!" (final price ₹1,200)
- If you want to advertise 45% or a ₹1,100 price point, you'd need to change the mechanics (e.g., 25% + 25%, or a flat 45%-off single discount) — not two discounts of 25% and 20% applied sequentially.

Risk: if this exact "45% / ₹1,100" framing already went out elsewhere (other channels, prior drafts), it's worth checking those too — this is a mechanical error that would repeat anywhere the same two percentages were combined additively.
