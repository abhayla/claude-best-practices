CASE-TAG: P02

Straight answer: as specified, this spec is internally contradictory — (a) and (b)+(c) cannot both hold, and I'd flag that rather than build something that quietly fails its own requirement.

**The conflict**

A once-per-minute poll gives a worst-case wait of ~60 seconds between an alert landing in the queue and the poller picking it up — before any processing, delivery, or retry time is added. That alone blows the 5-second SLA by more than 10x, for every single alert whose queue-arrival happens to fall just after a poll tick, not just an edge case. Constraint (c) rules out the standard fix (push/streaming), so there's no way to hit (a) within this quarter's stated limits. This isn't a tuning problem, it's a hard bound: polling interval is a floor on worst-case latency, and that floor is well above the SLA regardless of how fast everything else in the pipeline runs.

**What I'd do instead of building it anyway**

1. **Escalate the conflict now, not after launch.** One line to whoever owns the SLA: "5s alert delivery is not achievable on a 60s poll without push infra — need a decision on which constraint moves." This is a genuine intent fork (spec self-contradicts), worth a real answer rather than a guess.
2. **Give them the actual trade space** so the decision is a 30-second call, not a research project:
   - Relax the SLA to something a 60s poll can actually hit (e.g. "P95 within 90s") — cheapest, zero infra change.
   - Relax "no push infra this quarter" for the cheapest possible push mechanism — most queues already support long-poll or a lightweight notify callback (SQS long-polling, Postgres `LISTEN/NOTIFY`, Redis pub/sub) that isn't "streaming infrastructure" in any heavyweight sense — this might not even violate the spirit of (c) depending on what the org means by it.
   - Keep 60s polling but shrink effective latency by tightening the poll interval only for a high-priority subset (if the "5s" requirement is really about a subset of alerts, not all of them) — cheap if true, but I'd confirm before assuming it.
3. **Do NOT build a poller that silently produces ~30–90s p50 latency while the spec claims 5s.** That's the outcome that bites someone in production three months from now when a real incident alert takes a minute to page out and nobody knew the SLA was fiction.

**If forced to ship something today under the constraints as literally written**, the least-bad option is: poll every 1 minute (satisfies b+c), process the full batch in parallel with no artificial throttling, and publish the *honest* SLA as "delivered within 60–65s of queue arrival, not 5s" — with that number in the doc so at least nobody is misled. But I would not present that as meeting requirement (a); I'd present it as "here's what (b)+(c) actually buys you, and it isn't (a) — your call on which one to change."

**Assumption:** I'm reading "poll once per minute" as a fixed interval, not "poll, with 1-minute being an average across variable-rate polling" — if there's flexibility to poll much faster for a subset of high-priority alerts while keeping average cost down, that changes the answer (see option 3 above). Worth confirming which one is meant before anyone builds against this.
