Here's the explainer:

**Why slash commands get enhanced by design (even though the SSOT says they shouldn't)**

Short answer: they aren't, by design — this is a bug, not a feature. `enhance_slash_commands: false` is the single source of truth, and its intent is unambiguous: a typed `/command` is a fixed invocation, not free text to strengthen, so it should run exactly as-is.

What's actually happening in production is a **coexistence gap between two independent enforcement paths** that both think they own this decision:

1. The **installed plugin's own hook** (the SSOT's actual runtime enforcement).
2. A **separate operational enforcement layer** (an earlier or parallel hook/gate) that was written before the plugin became the single source of truth and still runs its own detection logic.

When both are wired, they don't always agree on what counts as "this turn was a slash command" — e.g., one layer inspects the raw prompt text pre-hook-normalization, the other post-normalization, or one fires later in the turn looking for expected side effects and doesn't know the turn was exempt at intake. The result: a slash-command turn that one layer correctly skipped gets flagged or re-routed by the other as if it were free text, and it visibly goes through the enhancement pipeline.

This is a known failure shape for "two enforcement points, one rule" systems generally — the fix pattern is a "coexistence stand-down": pick one layer as the sole enforcement point for `enhance_slash_commands`, and make the other a no-op (or exemption-only) whenever the primary is present, rather than having both independently gate the same decision.

**Action for the team:** if you're seeing slash commands enhanced in production, don't touch the SSOT (`enhance_slash_commands: false` is correct) — check whether a second hook/gate and the plugin's own enforcement are both active and disagreeing on slash-command detection. Treat it as a coexistence/dedup bug, not a config problem.
