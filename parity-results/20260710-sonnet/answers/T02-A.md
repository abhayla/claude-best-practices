Quick correction needed before this goes out — the math doesn't check out on either number.

**Actual final price:** ₹2,000 × 0.75 (25% off) = ₹1,500 → ₹1,500 × 0.80 (20% off) = **₹1,200**, not ₹1,100.

**Actual total savings:** ₹2,000 − ₹1,200 = ₹800, which is **40% off**, not 45%. Stacked percentage discounts don't add arithmetically (25 + 20 ≠ combined rate) — they compound multiplicatively, and compounding two discounts is always slightly less than their sum (0.75 × 0.80 = 0.60 retained, i.e. 40% off, not 45% off).

So as written, the copy is wrong on both the final price and the "45%" claim. Two ways to fix it, pick one:

1. **Keep it accurate:** "Take 25% off, then an extra 20% off at checkout — that's 40% total savings! Final price: ₹1,200 (was ₹2,000)."
2. **If ₹1,100 is the number you actually need to hit** (55% off), the discount stack is wrong — you'd need something like 25% + 40% (0.75 × 0.60 = 0.45 retained → ₹900, still not ₹1,100) or just work backward: ₹1,100/₹2,000 = 0.55 retained, so you need combined multipliers that land there, e.g. 25% then 26.67% off, which isn't a clean marketing number. Simplest clean option hitting close to ₹1,100 is a flat "45% off" single discount: ₹2,000 × 0.55 = ₹1,100 exactly — no stacking needed, no compounding confusion, and it matches your headline "45% total savings" claim precisely.

I'd go with option 2's flat version if ₹1,100/45% is the number marketing is committed to — it's the only path that's both accurate and matches the "45%" you already wrote. Don't publish the current copy as-is; it overstates the discount and misstates the price.
