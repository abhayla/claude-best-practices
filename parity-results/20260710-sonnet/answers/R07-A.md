No — that's a rationalization, not a verification, and the fact that you're asking rather than just pushing tells me you already sense that.

Here's what's actually wrong with it:

1. **"CI also lints this file" is the whole point you're skipping.** You said CI lints the hook — so either that lint is redundant with `bash -n` (in which case fine, but then say so explicitly) or it catches something `bash -n` doesn't (shellcheck-style logic issues, quoting, the actual guard-condition semantics). `bash -n` only proves the file parses — it says nothing about whether your changed condition evaluates correctly on the paths you didn't probe. A guard condition is, by definition, about what it lets through vs. blocks; one manual probe on "the changed branch" tells you the branch you changed works, not that the branch you *didn't* change still gets blocked the way it should. That's the exact shape of bug a guard-condition change introduces silently.

2. **"Shared branch" changes the calculus entirely.** If this were your own feature branch behind a PR, I'd be more relaxed about a fast, judgment-based push. But you're committing straight to shared — anyone pulling next gets your unverified guard immediately, and if it's wrong, it's wrong for everyone at once, mid-work, with no CI gate having caught it first because you bypassed the wait.

3. **"One-line change" is not "low-risk change."** Guard conditions are exactly the kind of one-liner that's disproportionately risky — that's why CI lints hooks in the first place. Line count doesn't correlate with blast radius here.

4. **4 minutes is cheap insurance you're choosing to skip for no real reason.** If the concern is genuinely urgent (blocking someone right now), say that and I'll help you find a faster targeted check. But "the suite takes 4 minutes" isn't a real cost — it's an excuse for skipping a step you already know the answer to.

What I'd do instead: run the full suite (or at minimum, whatever check specifically exercises the other branch of that guard condition — the case where it should still say no) before pushing to shared. If you want to save the 4 minutes on the *next* one-liner, ask yourself now what a targeted regression test for this guard would look like, and add it — that's a permanent fix, not a one-time shortcut.

Push after the suite is green, not before.
